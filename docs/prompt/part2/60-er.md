============================================================
60. ENTITY RESOLUTION: ALGORITHM, QUALITY CONTRACT AND UNCERTAINTY
============================================================

60.0 POSITION AND OVERRIDES

Entity resolution (ER) is the largest soundness risk in SPECTRA. Every fact the kernel grounds is
indexed by an `EntityId`; every corridor, every cut, every certificate is a statement about those
entities. One false merge fabricates a causal link that never existed, ECLIPSE then proves a
theorem about the fabricated chain, and the certificate is internally valid and externally wrong.
The Go checker cannot catch it: it shares the ER output, not an independent derivation of it.

OVERRIDES Part I: ECLIPSE §2 describes `bundle.jsonl` as "entity-resolved telemetry" as if
resolution were a given. It is not. ER is a named, versioned, hashed pipeline stage with its own
input, its own output artifact, its own determinism gates and its own published error rates.

OVERRIDES Part I sections 9-12: `EntityId` is not author-assigned and is not a closed catalog. It
is *derived* and content-addressed (60.5). The catalog of `EntityKind` is closed; the catalog of
`EntityId` is an output.

OVERRIDES Part I / ECLIPSE §5: the certificate gains an `er` block (60.9) and the Go checker gains
four ER obligations (60.11). `hashes` gains `er_config` and `er_output`.

OVERRIDES Part I / ECLIPSE §9: the sentence "soundness is relative to the entity resolution" stops
being a disclaimer and becomes a measured, published quantity (60.10), with a verdict state
(`ROBUST-MODULO-ER`) that makes the dependency structurally visible instead of footnoted.

ER is a pure function. No clock, no RNG, no environment read, no network, no database:

```
er_resolve : (bundle_raw.jsonl, er.toml) -> (er.jsonl, er-mergelog.jsonl, er-report.json)
```

OVERRIDES Part I sections 10.6 and 12.1: ER's declared input of (ordered canonical events, source
registry, realm profiles), with identifiers pre-extracted by adapters into `raw_identifiers`, is
replaced by the two-input signature above; `spectra-er` performs its own extraction from the
per-source field paths declared in `er.toml` (60.7) and is therefore not subject to the section
10.1 import lint that makes non-adapter code reading a vendor format a build failure. An
implementer following Part I builds ER over `CanonicalEvent.raw_identifiers` and has nowhere to
read the `[crossref]` field pairs that `B7` (60.3) and rules 11-15 (60.4) require.

Same bytes in, same bytes out, on every machine, forever. Enforced by G-ER-1 and G-ER-2 (60.12).

60.1 IDENTIFIER ALGEBRA

Every identifier observed in telemetry is typed, normalized, and assigned an *evidential class*.
The class, not the string, decides whether the identifier can cause a merge.

Classes:

- `ANCHOR`  — issued by a trusted authority inside the modeled estate, not chooseable by a
              principal under attack. May cause a merge on its own.
- `LINK`    — stable and meaningful, but reused, guessable, or issued by a component the attacker
              may influence. May cause a merge only with a corroborating ANCHOR or a second
              independent LINK, and only inside a validity interval.
- `HINT`    — attacker-writable, cosmetic, or derived from free text. May be used for blocking,
              display and explanation. MAY NEVER CAUSE A MERGE. This is the ER-poisoning defense
              (60.13) and it is enforced by a type-level lint, not by convention.

OVERRIDES Part I section 12.2: the three identifier strength tiers (Tier A authoritative and
merging on its own, Tier B strong-local and merging within a realm that declares the class unique,
Tier C never alone) are replaced by the three evidential classes above, with different memberships
and different merge licenses. `HOST_NAME` is `HINT` and may never cause a merge, where Part I
places `host_ref` in Tier B; `UID_POSIX`, `CONTAINER_ID`, `MAC` and `DEVICE_ID` are `LINK` and
require a corroborating `ANCHOR` or a second independent `LINK`, where Part I places `uid` and
`container_ref` in Tier A and merges on either one alone. An implementer following Part I's Tier A
and Tier B licenses performs exactly the hostname and bare-uid merges that the ER-POISON-01
fixture (60.13) requires to produce nothing.

| `SID_WINDOWS`      | ANCHOR | realm        | never            | unbounded (birth event if observed)     |

OVERRIDES Part I section 10.2: the closed 19-value `RawIdentifier.class` enum (username, upn,
email, uid, gid, sid, account_ref, token_hash, cred_hash, session_ref, device_ref, host_ref,
container_ref, ip, mac, cert_fp, pid, service_name, resource_ref) is replaced by the `IdKind`
catalog above. Neither catalog contains the other: `gid`, `email`, `account_ref`, `cred_hash`,
`service_name` and `resource_ref` have no `IdKind` and are extracted by nothing, and
`OBJECT_GUID`, `CLIENT_ID`, `POD_UID`, `IMAGE_DIGEST` and `PROCESS_KEY` cannot be carried in a
section 10.2 `raw_identifiers` array without a MAJOR schema bump. An implementer following Part I
writes adapters that emit classes this stage will not consume.

`PROCESS_KEY` is the triple `(host_entity, pid, start_time_ns)` — never the bare pid. A bare pid is
a `HINT`.

OVERRIDES Part I section 12.3: rule R8, which attaches a `process.*` record carrying only a bare
pid to the unique process with `start <= t < exit` and yields UNRESOLVED when that process is not
unique, is replaced by rule 26 `process_key` (60.4), which requires an identical
`(host_entity, pid, start_time_ns)` triple; a bare pid is a `HINT`, is skipped before rule
evaluation (60.7), and joins no component at all. An implementer following Part I resolves a
bare-pid record by time-window lookup, which is the pid-reuse attachment the triple exists to
prevent.

Normalization is per-kind, declared in `er.toml`, and applied before any comparison. Normalization
is itself an attack surface (ERF-6), so it is deliberately narrow:

- `USERNAME`, `UPN_EMAIL`: Unicode NFKC, then ASCII-lowercase only (NOT full Unicode casefold —
  full casefold maps distinct codepoints together and manufactures collisions). Any identifier
  containing a codepoint outside `[\x21-\x7e]` after NFKC is emitted with
  `normalization = NON_ASCII` and demoted one class (ANCHOR->LINK, LINK->HINT).
- Email local-part sub-addressing (`+tag`) is NOT stripped. Dot-folding is NOT applied. Both are
  provider-specific and both are attacker-chooseable.
- `SID_WINDOWS`, `TOKEN_JTI`, `API_KEY_ID`, `SESSION_ID`, digests: byte-exact, no case change.
- `HOST_NAME`: NFKC, ASCII-lowercase, trailing dot stripped, IDN punycode preserved as written.
- `IP_ADDR`: parsed to a 16-byte representation; IPv4-mapped IPv6 normalized to IPv4.
- `MAC`: lowercase hex, colon-separated.

OVERRIDES Part I sections 9.3 and 10.6: NFC normalization with case-folding applied only to fields
that a realm profile declares case-insensitive is replaced by the per-kind NFKC plus
ASCII-lowercase rules above, declared in `er.toml` and applied uniformly before every comparison;
the realm profile's case-sensitivity declaration is not an input to this stage. An implementer
following Part I computes a different normal form for the same raw identifier, and therefore a
different partition and different content-addressed `EntityId`s (60.5) from the same bundle.

A normalization that maps two byte-distinct raw identifiers to the same normal form emits a
`NORM_COLLISION` record carrying both raw forms and both EventIds. Collisions are published in
`er-report.json` and are never silently resolved.

60.2 BINDINGS AND TEMPORAL VALIDITY

An identifier does not belong to an entity; a *binding* does, over an interval.

```rust
#[derive(Clone, PartialEq, Eq, PartialOrd, Ord)]   // Ord is the deterministic sort key
pub struct Binding {
    pub kind:      IdKind,
    pub norm:      IdBytes,          // normalized identifier, byte-exact
    pub raw:       IdBytes,          // as observed
    pub t_lo:      Tick,             // inclusive, integer ticks (Part II determinism charter)
    pub t_hi:      Tick,             // exclusive; Tick::MAX == open
    pub edge_lo:   EdgeKind,         // WITNESSED | INFERRED | PROVISIONAL
    pub edge_hi:   EdgeKind,
    pub source:    SourceId,
    pub evidence:  Vec<EventId>,     // sorted, deduplicated, non-empty
    pub class:     EvidenceClass,    // ANCHOR | LINK | HINT (after demotion rules)
}
```

`EdgeKind` semantics, and the fail-closed rule that binds ER to the liveness machinery
(ECLIPSE §4A):

- `WITNESSED` — the interval endpoint is an ingested event (lease grant, token issue, container
  create, logout).
- `INFERRED`  — the endpoint follows from a declared, hashed policy constant in `er.toml`
  (e.g. token `exp` claim, DHCP lease duration carried in the record itself).
- `PROVISIONAL` — the endpoint is unknown because the source that would emit it is BLIND or
  SUPPRESSED over the relevant window.

HARD RULE (fail-closed): a merge whose licensing interval overlap depends on a `PROVISIONAL`
endpoint MAY NOT produce `RESOLVED`. It produces `AMBIGUOUS`. Blindness may never be converted
into confidence. OVERRIDES Part I: Part I lets missing telemetry widen the hypothesis space in the
kernel while leaving ER free to guess; both stages now fail closed on the same liveness artifact.

Interval overlap used by the match rules:

```
overlap(a, b)  ::=  max(a.t_lo, b.t_lo) < min(a.t_hi, b.t_hi)
adjacent(a, b) ::=  a.t_hi == b.t_lo  AND  a.edge_hi == WITNESSED  AND  b.edge_lo == WITNESSED
```

`adjacent` licenses continuity across a witnessed handoff (a token rotation, a session renewal).
Nothing else licenses continuity. There is no slack parameter, no epsilon, no "close enough".

OVERRIDES Part I sections 12.5 and 12.8: the requirement that a rotated credential never merge
across the rotation boundary — two entities with disjoint validity intervals,
`rotation_generation` incremented, a `succeeded_by` edge, and a mandatory regression test asserting
that a rotation does not merge generations — is replaced by `adjacent` together with rule 13
`token_rotation` (60.4), which merges the two `REFRESH_TOKEN_ID` bindings into one entity across a
witnessed handoff. An implementer following Part I keeps a rotation test that rule 13 fails, and
gives `rotation_generation` and `succeeded_by` a role that ER output does not have.

60.3 CANDIDATE GENERATION AND BLOCKING

Blocking exists only to bound comparisons. A blocking key NEVER causes a merge and never appears in
a merge justification. This is asserted by a unit test that runs the matcher with blocking disabled
(full O(n^2) comparison) on every fixture and requires a byte-identical partition (G-ER-9).

Blocking keys, computed per binding, emitted as `BlockKey(u8 key_id, IdBytes)`:

```
B1  exact_norm         (kind, norm)                    -- all kinds
B2  realm_local        (realm_id, local_part(norm))    -- UPN_EMAIL, USERNAME, SERVICE_ACCOUNT
B3  host_scoped        (host_entity, kind, norm)       -- UID_POSIX, PROCESS_KEY, CONTAINER_ID
B4  id_prefix12        (kind, norm[0..12])             -- CONTAINER_ID (docker short-id reality)
B5  net_lease          (vrf_id, ip_bytes, lease_epoch) -- IP_ADDR
B6  token_family       (idp_id, client_id)             -- TOKEN_JTI, REFRESH_TOKEN_ID, SESSION_ID
B7  cross_ref          (kind_a, norm_a, kind_b, norm_b) from co-occurrence in one record
```

`B7` is generated only from a single telemetry record that names two identifiers in declared
fields — for example an IdP token-issue record naming both `sub` and `jti`. The field pairs that
produce `B7` are enumerated in `er.toml` per source schema; a pair not enumerated there produces no
candidate. Co-occurrence in free text produces nothing.

Candidate pair cap: `max_candidates_per_block` in `er.toml`. If a block exceeds it, the block is
NOT truncated — the whole block is marked `BLOCK_OVERFLOW`, every binding in it is marked
`AMBIGUOUS`, and the run carries the `er_block_overflow` flag. OVERRIDES Part I: caps in Part I
silently drop work; here a cap degrades to uncertainty, never to a guess.

60.4 THE ORDERED DETERMINISTIC MATCH RULES

Rules are total, side-effect-free predicates over a candidate pair of bindings `(a, b)`. They are
evaluated in ordinal order. Each rule emits exactly one of `MERGE`, `EXCLUDE`, `AMBIG`, `PASS`.
The first non-`PASS` verdict wins for that pair; no rule below it is consulted. Rule ordinals are
stable across releases: a rule is never renumbered, only retired (its ordinal is burned and listed
in `er.toml` under `retired_rules`). Ordinals are hashed into `er_config`.

OVERRIDES Part I section 12.3: the rule catalog `R1`..`R14`, evaluated in fixed order and recorded
on every resolved reference as `resolved_by_rule` matching `^R[0-9]{1,2}$` (section 10.2), is
replaced by the ordinal-keyed table below; an applied merge cites its `rule_ord` in
`er-mergelog.jsonl` and the checker verifies that ordinal against the hashed `[rules] enabled`
list (60.11). An implementer following Part I emits `R`-prefixed rule ids that no checker
obligation can validate, and Part I's R3, R8, R10, R11, R12 and R13 have no ordinal in this table
at all.

is a binding on the subject entity and not an entity, and the resulting component is reported with
kind `PRINCIPAL` (60.15), which Part I section 9.2 forbids as a kind. An implementer following
Part I builds a typed union-find that merges only within a kind, which makes rules 11-15
unimplementable, and retains credential and session edges with no endpoints in this partition.

OVERRIDES Part I sections 12.2 and 12.8: the Tier-C prohibition on merging two entities on an
address alone, the lint that rejects any rule consuming a Tier-C identifier as its sole basis, and
the structural NAT-collapse defense built on that prohibition are replaced by rule 30
`ip_within_lease` above, which merges on a matching `IP_ADDR` in one vrf when both bindings fall
inside a single lease with `WITNESSED` or `INFERRED` endpoints — the lease interval, not a second
identifier, supplies the corroboration. An implementer following Part I lints rule 30 out of the
build and leaves `IP_ADDR` unable to license any merge.

OVERRIDES Part I section 12.3: rule R3, which links `username` to `sid` whenever one
`authentication.*` record carries both in the same realm, has no ordinal in the table above and is
not reinstated elsewhere; a `USERNAME` and `SID_WINDOWS` co-occurrence matches no MERGE rule, so
`USERNAME` merges only through rule 21 (both sides already sharing an ANCHOR component) or rule 22
(a second independent LINK), and otherwise reaches rule 41 `username_one_link` -> `AMBIG`. An
implementer following Part I merges an account name into its SID on a single logon record, which is
how the Windows 4624 worked example of Part I section 10.8 resolves there and not here.

There are no scores, no thresholds, no weights, no learned models, and no approximate string
metrics anywhere in this table or in the code that implements it. See 60.14.

Rules 00-03 (`EXCLUDE`) are evaluated over *components*, not only over the originating pair: an
exclusion recorded between two bindings propagates to their components and persists. A later MERGE
proposal that would join two components separated by a standing exclusion does not apply and does
not fail — it produces `AMBIG` (60.6).

60.5 UNION-FIND WITH DETERMINISTIC MERGE ORDER

```rust
pub struct Resolver {
    parent:    Vec<u32>,            // union-find over BindingIx
    rank:      Vec<u8>,             // internal only; NEVER decides the representative
    excl:      BTreeSet<(u32,u32)>, // canonicalized component-pair exclusions
    ambig:     BTreeMap<u32, BTreeSet<AmbiguityId>>,
    log:       Vec<MergeRecord>,    // append-only, emitted as er-mergelog.jsonl
}

pub struct Proposal {
    rule_ord: u8,
    t_key:    Tick,        // min(a.t_lo, b.t_lo)
    pair_key: [u8; 64],    // BLAKE3 of the canonical encoding of (a, b), a<b by Binding::Ord
    a: u32, b: u32,
}
```

Proposals are generated per block, collected into one `Vec`, then sorted by
`(rule_ord, t_key, pair_key)` — a total order with no ties, since `pair_key` is unique per pair.
They are applied in that order. Sorting is a stable sort over an already-total key; parallel
generation is permitted, parallel *application* is forbidden.

Representative selection does NOT depend on merge order. After the last proposal, the exported
`EntityId` of a component is derived from the component's content only:

```
EntityKey(component) = BLAKE3( concat over sorted(bindings) of canonical_encode(binding) )
EntityId             = "ent:" || hex(EntityKey[0..16])
```

`canonical_encode` is the canonical serialization from the Part II certificate-format section
(fixed field order, LEB128-free fixed-width integers, no floats, length-prefixed byte strings).
Consequence: `EntityId` is content-addressed. Two runs that produce the same partition produce the
same ids; a run that resolves differently produces visibly different ids rather than silently
reusing one. The display label shown in the UI is chosen by a documented preference order over
kinds and is decorative — it is never an identity.

Confluence requirement (G-ER-1): for `N` (declared in `er.toml`, minimum 64) permutations of the
raw record order and of the block iteration order, `er.jsonl` must be byte-identical. A property
test asserts this on every fixture. `HashMap`/`HashSet` are banned from the entire `spectra-er`
crate by a clippy lint plus a symbol-level check in CI; `BTreeMap`/`BTreeSet`/sorted `Vec` only.

60.6 THE THREE OUTCOMES

```
                          ┌──────────────┐
   candidate pairs  ──▶   │  match rules │
                          └──────┬───────┘
                MERGE ◀──────────┼──────────▶ EXCLUDE
                   │             │ AMBIG            │
                   ▼             ▼                  ▼
            ┌────────────┐  ┌──────────┐    ┌──────────────┐
            │ union-find │  │ ambiguity│    │  exclusion   │
            │  component │  │  record  │    │   edge       │
            └─────┬──────┘  └────┬─────┘    └──────┬───────┘
                  │              │                 │
                  ▼              ▼                 ▼
             RESOLVED       AMBIGUOUS         (prevents merge;
             (has ANCHOR    (>=1 open         may itself yield
              or 2 LINK)     ambiguity)        AMBIGUOUS later)
                  │
                  ▼  no rule fired at all, singleton component
             UNRESOLVED
```

`EntityStatus` is a closed, three-valued type. It is not a score and it may not be flattened:

```rust
#[derive(Clone, Copy, PartialEq, Eq, PartialOrd, Ord)]
pub enum EntityStatus { Resolved, Ambiguous, Unresolved }
```

OVERRIDES Part I sections 9.4 and 10.2: the four-valued `resolution_state` enum
`RESOLVED | PROVISIONAL | UNRESOLVED | SPLIT_SUSPECT` is replaced by the three-valued
`EntityStatus` above. `PROVISIONAL` and `SPLIT_SUSPECT` are not entity statuses here, `AMBIGUOUS`
is new, and `PROVISIONAL` denotes only an interval endpoint (60.2). An implementer following
Part I emits `resolution_state` values this type cannot represent and has no status in which to
record a fork, while `AMBIGUOUS` fails the section 10.2 `additionalProperties:false` schema.

- `RESOLVED` — the component is joined by at least one `ANCHOR` merge, or by at least two
  independent `LINK` merges over overlapping witnessed intervals, and carries no open ambiguity.
- `AMBIGUOUS` — first-class. The component carries at least one `AmbiguityId`: a recorded pair (or
  set) of alternative resolutions that the evidence does not separate. AMBIGUOUS is *not* a merge
  and *not* a refusal to merge; it is a recorded fork. The component stays as it is, and every
  alternative is enumerated in `er.jsonl`.
- `UNRESOLVED` — a singleton with no qualifying merge. Usable, addressable, and explicitly not
  claimed to be anyone. `UNRESOLVED` is not an error and is not a failure; a run consisting largely
  of `UNRESOLVED` entities at 30% completeness is the honest outcome, and 60.10 publishes it.

OVERRIDES Part I section 12.6: the separate unresolved identifier space
`"unres-" base32(BLAKE3_128(scenario_id LF kind_guess LF sorted_raw_identifiers))` and the closed
reason-code set `NO_RULE_MATCHED | AMBIGUOUS_PID | KIND_AMBIGUOUS | SPLIT_SUSPECT | REALM_UNKNOWN`
are replaced by an ordinary `ent:`-prefixed content-addressed id (60.5) carrying no reason code and
no `kind_guess`. An implementer following Part I builds a UI that renders "unresolved: <reason>"
from fields `er.jsonl` does not carry, and any consumer keying off the `unres-` prefix matches
nothing.

```json
{"ambiguity_id":"amb:9c1e0f42","rule_ord":44,"kind":"USERNAME","norm":"svc_deploy",
 "alternatives":[{"entity":"ent:3a0c…","realm":"corp.local"},
                 {"entity":"ent:71bd…","realm":"dmz.local"}],
 "evidence":["evt:000481","evt:000497"],

```

`resolvable_by` names the (source, window) pairs whose liveness would decide the fork. It is
computed by the same machinery as ECLIPSE §4G and reported in the same place.

PROPAGATION. Ambiguity is carried, never dropped:

1. Every fact grounded from a binding in an AMBIGUOUS component carries
   `er_status = AMBIGUOUS` and the set of `AmbiguityId`s that reach it.
2. A rule instance inherits the union of the `AmbiguityId`s of its body facts and its head fact.
3. A corridor inherits the union over its instances.
4. The certificate lists, per corridor, the ambiguities that touch it.

There is no step at which an ambiguity is resolved by picking the more likely branch, the earlier
branch, the larger component, or the branch that makes the proof work. There is no tie-break here.
Tie-breaking is correct for *presentation order* (Part II, solver well-definedness) and forbidden
for *identity*.

OVERRIDES Part I section 12.5: the conflict policy that resolves a cardinality conflict by dropping
the union edge with the highest rule number, then the later witness timestamp, then the
lexicographically larger witness `EventId`, and resolves a type conflict by splitting at the
lowest-tier union edge into two `SPLIT_SUSPECT` components, is replaced by the recorded fork above:
the component is left as it is and carries an `AmbiguityId`. An implementer following Part I ships
exactly the rule-number, recency and provenance tie-breaks that 60.14(4) forbids and that the lint
and the reviewers are instructed to reject.

60.7 ALGORITHM (NORMATIVE PSEUDOCODE)

```
fn er_resolve(raw: &[Record], cfg: &ErConfig, liveness: &Liveness) -> ErOutput {
    // 1. Extract and normalize. Pure, per-record, order-independent.
    let mut bindings: Vec<Binding> = Vec::new();
    for rec in raw {                                   // raw is already in canonical ingest order
        for field in cfg.extractors_for(rec.source, rec.schema_version) {
            let b = normalize(field.kind, rec.get(field.path), cfg)?;   // may emit NORM_COLLISION
            let b = bound_interval(b, rec, cfg, liveness);              // sets edge_lo/edge_hi
            let b = demote_class(b, cfg);                               // NON_ASCII, HINT kinds
            bindings.push(b);
        }
    }
    bindings.sort();            // Binding::Ord — total, byte-level
    bindings.dedup();

    // 2. Blocking. Keys B1..B7. No merges here.
    let blocks: BTreeMap<BlockKey, Vec<BindingIx>> = build_blocks(&bindings, cfg);

    // 3. Proposal generation. Rules 00..99 in ordinal order, per block.
    let mut proposals: Vec<Proposal> = Vec::new();
    let mut overflow:  BTreeSet<BlockKey> = BTreeSet::new();
    for (key, ixs) in &blocks {
        if ixs.len() > cfg.max_candidates_per_block { overflow.insert(key.clone()); continue; }
        for (i, j) in ordered_pairs(ixs) {             // i < j by Binding::Ord
            if bindings[i].class == HINT || bindings[j].class == HINT { continue; }   // 60.14
            match first_firing_rule(&bindings[i], &bindings[j], cfg) {
                Verdict::Merge(ord)   => proposals.push(Proposal::merge(ord, i, j, &bindings)),
                Verdict::Exclude(ord) => proposals.push(Proposal::exclude(ord, i, j, &bindings)),
                Verdict::Ambig(ord)   => proposals.push(Proposal::ambig(ord, i, j, &bindings)),
                Verdict::Pass         => {}
            }
        }
    }

    // 4. Deterministic application.
    proposals.sort_by_key(|p| (p.rule_ord, p.t_key, p.pair_key));   // total order
    let mut r = Resolver::new(bindings.len());
    for p in &proposals {
        match p.kind {
            Exclude => r.add_exclusion(p),                      // applied first by ordinal 00..03
            Merge   => if r.exclusion_between(p.a, p.b) {
                           r.record_ambiguity(p, AmbigCause::ExclusionConflict);
                       } else if r.same_component(p.a, p.b) {
                           r.log_redundant(p);
                       } else {
                           r.union(p);                          // logs MergeRecord
                       },
            Ambig   => r.record_ambiguity(p, AmbigCause::Rule(p.rule_ord)),
        }
    }
    for key in &overflow { r.mark_block_ambiguous(key); }

    // 5. Status assignment and content-addressed ids.
    r.finalize(cfg)     // EntityStatus per component, EntityKey = BLAKE3(sorted canonical bindings)
}
```

Complexity: `O(sum over blocks of |block|^2)` proposals, bounded by `max_candidates_per_block`;
union-find near-linear; the sort dominates in practice. The step budget is a *deterministic
instruction budget*, never a wall-clock timeout (Part II determinism charter). Budget exhaustion
marks every unprocessed block `BLOCK_OVERFLOW` and therefore AMBIGUOUS — identically on every
machine.

60.10 THE QUALITY CONTRACT

OVERRIDES Part I: Part I never measures ER. ER quality is now measured at EVERY cell of the
degradation matrix (completeness axis x operator axis, per the Part II degradation section), on
HELD-OUT scenarios under a frozen rules hash, and published in the benchmark artifact.

Ground truth. The generator emits `ground-truth/principals.jsonl`: for every emitted record field
that carries an identifier, the true principal id that the generator used. The ER evaluator joins
predicted components to ground-truth principals through the *pre-degradation* record index, which
the degradation harness preserves as `record_lineage.jsonl` (records deleted or corrupted by an
operator keep their lineage entry with a `removed` marker). This is what makes the metric
measurable under `suppress` and `corrupt` — the defect the feasibility critic identified.

Metric definitions (fixed here, pre-registered, not chooseable after seeing results):

- Pair universe `U` = all unordered pairs of *surviving* identifier-observations that ground truth
  assigns to some principal. Pairs involving removed records are excluded from `U` and counted
  separately as `lost_pairs`.
- `TP` = pairs in `U` with same GT principal and same predicted component.
- `FP` = pairs in `U` with different GT principals and same predicted component.
- `FN` = pairs in `U` with same GT principal and different predicted components.
- `pairwise_precision = TP/(TP+FP)`, `pairwise_recall = TP/(TP+FN)`,
  `pairwise_f1 = 2PR/(P+R)`. Undefined denominators are reported as `null`, never as 0 and never
  as 1.
- `false_merge_components` = predicted components containing >=2 GT principals (count, not rate).
- `false_split_principals` = GT principals spread over >=2 predicted components.
- `ambiguity_rate` = |AMBIGUOUS entities| / |entities|.
- `unresolved_rate` = |UNRESOLVED entities| / |entities|.
- `corridor_er_error` = fraction of facts in enumerated corridors whose entity is a false merge or
  false split. THIS IS THE METRIC THAT MATTERS; the others are context.
- `verdict_flip_rate` = from the injection experiment below.

Pairs are counted over *observations*, not over entities, so a single hub entity cannot dominate.
Every cell is run with the declared number of seeds; median and IQR are reported. A single run per
cell is not a result.

INJECTION EXPERIMENT (required, published). For each held-out scenario, inject `k` synthetic false
merges and `k` synthetic false splits into the ER output — directly, post-hoc, bypassing the
matcher — re-run the kernel, and record how often the safety field changes and how often the
reported cut changes. This yields the sensitivity of the headline result to ER error, which is the
number a reviewer will ask for. It is published as `bench/er-sensitivity.json` and summarized in
`LIMITATIONS.md`.

ANOMALY RULE (the self-flattering-degradation trap). Deleting records can *remove* the evidence of
a conflict and thereby lower `ambiguity_rate` while visibility gets worse — the same pathology the
critics found in the self-calibrated q99 liveness threshold. Therefore: if `ambiguity_rate` or
`false_split_principals` decreases as completeness decreases in any monotone slice, the build FAILS
unless an entry exists in `docs/research/er-anomalies.md` naming the cell, the mechanism and the
evidence. Additionally, `evidence_support` (mean count of corroborating EventIds per applied merge)
MUST be non-increasing as completeness decreases; a violation fails the build with no ledger
escape.

60.11 CHECKER OBLIGATIONS AND WHAT THEY DO NOT ESTABLISH

The Go checker does NOT re-run the matcher. Re-implementing blocking, normalization and 26 match
rules in Go would be a third full implementation of one semantics and would still share the rule
table, so the independence gained is small and the maintenance cost is large. OVERRIDES Part I: the
scope of checker independence with respect to ER is stated here precisely and is stated in
`docs/checker-scope.md`, in these words.

The checker MUST verify:

1. `BLAKE3(er.jsonl) == cert.er.output_hash` and `BLAKE3(er.toml) == cert.er.config_hash_bytes`.
2. Every `EntityId` referenced by any fact, instance, corridor, invariant or witness in the
   certificate exists in `er.jsonl`.
3. MERGE-LOG REPLAY: re-apply `er-mergelog.jsonl` into a fresh union-find in the recorded order;
   the resulting component partition must hash to the `EntityKey` set in `er.jsonl`; every merge
   record must cite a `rule_ord` that is enabled in the hashed config and `EventId`s that exist in
   the bundle. Replaying a log is not reimplementing a matcher, so this check is cheap, honest, and
   genuinely independent of the Rust union-find.
4. ALGEBRA: if `cert.safety == ROBUST`, then `cert.er.corridor_ambiguities` is empty AND no entity
   referenced by a corridor fact has status `AMBIGUOUS` in `er.jsonl` AND no `ErFlags` bit is set.
   Otherwise REJECT.

`docs/checker-scope.md` must state: a passing checker does NOT establish that the merges were
*correct*. It establishes that the merges that were applied are the merges that were recorded, that
they cite real events and enabled rules, that the partition matches, and that the verdict respects
the ER algebra. Correctness of merges is evidenced only by 60.10, only on held-out scenarios, and
only as a measured rate.

60.13 FAILURE TAXONOMY AND THE ER-POISONING RED TEAM

| Code    | Failure                       | Mechanism                                                          | Structural defense                                  |
|---------|-------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------|
| ERF-1   | False merge (over-merge)      | two principals joined by a reused or forged identifier              | class system; rules 00-03; 60.10 G-ER-4             |
| ERF-2   | False split                   | evidence linking two bindings deleted by degradation                | measured, not prevented; `unresolved_rate` published|

RED-TEAM CASE `ER-POISON-01` — forced merge by chosen identifier. Committed fixture, held-out,
must never produce `ROBUST`.

Attacker scenario, at the telemetry level only (no exploit code, per the project posture section):
the attacker controls a low-privilege account in `dmz.local`. It performs three telemetry-visible
acts: (a) sets its own account `displayName` to the display name of the privileged service account
`svc_deploy`; (b) creates a local POSIX user named `svc_deploy` on a host it already holds; (c)
sets the hostname of a container it launches to the `DEVICE_ID` string of the victim workstation.
The intent is to make ER fuse the attacker principal with `svc_deploy`, so that later privileged
actions by the real `svc_deploy` appear in the attacker's chain — inflating the reconstructed
attack, moving the corridor, and changing the cut.

Required behavior, asserted by `tests/er/redteam/er_poison_01.rs`:

- (a) produces nothing. `DISPLAY_NAME` is `HINT`; HINT pairs are dropped before rule evaluation and
  never appear in a merge justification.
- (b) produces rule 41 (`username_one_link`) -> `AMBIGUOUS`, not rule 22, because the corroborating
  `UID_POSIX` is host-scoped to the attacker's host and does not overlap the real SA's `UID_POSIX`
  binding, and because the real `svc_deploy` carries a `SERVICE_ACCOUNT` ANCHOR that the attacker
  account does not. An ambiguity record is emitted naming both alternatives and naming
  `idp_audit` over the relevant window as `resolvable_by`.
- (c) produces nothing. `HOST_NAME` and `CONTAINER_NAME` are `HINT`.
- The run's safety field is `ROBUST_MODULO_ER` at best, never `ROBUST`, because the ambiguity from
  (b) reaches a corridor fact.
- The UI renders the ambiguity as a fork with both alternatives and the `resolvable_by` source, and
  renders no merged entity.

60.14 NEGATIVE REQUIREMENTS AND FORBIDDEN CLAIMS

Do not, anywhere in the ER stage or downstream of it:

1. Use approximate string matching of any kind — Levenshtein, Jaro-Winkler, Soundex, Metaphone,
   n-gram similarity, embeddings, cosine distance. Enforced by G-ER-3 as a symbol-reachability
   check over the build graph, not by review.
2. Attach a score, probability, confidence, likelihood, weight, or threshold to a merge. There are
   three outcomes and they are categorical. A schema lint rejects any numeric field named
   `score`, `confidence`, `probability`, `similarity`, `weight` or `severity` anywhere in the ER
   schemas, the API, the database or the frontend.
3. Use an LLM, or any model, to propose, confirm, rank or explain a merge. The narrator may read
   `er.jsonl` and restate what it says; it may not decide anything. Removing the LLM must leave
   `er.jsonl` byte-identical (`make verify-no-llm`).
4. Resolve an ambiguity by any tie-break, heuristic, recency preference, size preference, or
   "most likely" argument. An ambiguity is resolved by evidence or it is carried.
5. Merge on a `HINT` identifier, on a single `LINK` identifier without corroboration, or across a
   `PROVISIONAL` interval endpoint.
6. Silently drop a record, a binding or a candidate pair. Every drop is a quarantine record with a
   reason code (Part II ingestion section). Silent drops manufacture blind windows, blind windows
   manufacture licenses, and licenses change the verdict.
7. Emit `ROBUST` on a run with any corridor ambiguity or any `ErFlags` bit set.
8. Persist the entity partition in Postgres as the source of truth. `er.jsonl` on disk is the
   truth; Postgres holds an index of it for query and nothing else.
9. Let `EntityId` depend on merge order, iteration order, thread count, locale, timezone, or host.

Forbidden claims, in README, docs, UI, paper, commit messages and CV bullets:

- "correctly identifies entities", "accurate entity resolution", "99% accurate ER", or any accuracy
  claim not carrying a result id from `bench/er-quality.json` and the split it came from.
- "resolves identities across sources" without the scope qualifier "under the declared identifier
  catalog and the generator's identifier semantics".
- any suggestion that ER precision measured against a generator's ground truth transfers to real
  telemetry. It does not. `LIMITATIONS.md` must state that the generator emits the identifier kinds
  the extractor knows about, that real estates contain kinds and collisions that are not modeled,
  and that measured ER quality is therefore an upper bound on an unmeasured real-world quality.
- "proves the attack chain" on any run whose safety field is `ROBUST_MODULO_ER`.
